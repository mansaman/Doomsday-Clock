# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People with ADHD (and anyone with time blindness) who lose track of how much of the day is actually theirs. They open the app many times a day, usually for a few seconds: to glance at free time left, dump a thought into a note, start a timer, or check their top three tasks. They are often mid-distraction when they open it, so every screen must be readable in one glance.

## Product Purpose

Doomsday Clock treats time as currency, inspired by the film *In Time*. Sleep, work, and commute are debited up front; what remains is the free time you actually own today, shown draining in real time. A life bank (remaining lifespan) gives the long view. Tasks, notes, flash reminders, alarms, and timers turn that awareness into action. Success is the user noticing time passing and choosing what to spend it on, without the app itself becoming a distraction.

## Brand Personality

Calm, honest, quietly urgent. The interface is composed and Apple-native by default, like a well-made system utility. Urgency is earned, not constant: color and emphasis escalate only as free time genuinely runs low. Copy is short, sentence case, direct, never shouting or shaming.

## Anti-references

- Shouty all-caps "hustle" copy and guilt-tripping messages.
- Horror or blood-red "doom" theatrics as the default state.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: hero metric with gradient accent, identical icon-card grids.
- Glass used as noise: stacked blurs, glowing borders everywhere, neon on black.

## Design Principles

1. **One glance, one answer.** The home screen answers "how much time is mine?" before anything else.
2. **Urgency is a signal, not a mood.** Calm surfaces by default; red appears only when time is actually short.
3. **Native feel earns trust.** Follow Apple conventions (materials, type, motion) so the tool disappears into the task.
4. **Low friction over features.** Capturing a note or starting a timer takes one tap from home.
5. **Respect attention.** Motion conveys state only; nothing loops just to look alive.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, including text on glass surfaces (glass must keep enough opacity to hold contrast over any backdrop). Honor reduced-motion settings (disable ambient backdrop motion and pulsing). Never rely on color alone for urgency: pair it with text and numbers. Large tap targets (44pt minimum) for quick, distracted use.
